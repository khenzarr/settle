# Settle mark exploration — D4D0.6B1

**Status:** design exploration only. No candidate is a final Settle logo. Final selection requires human visual review.

## 1. Exploration goal

This study explores six structurally distinct black-and-white marks inside the authoritative **Convergent Ledger** territory. Settle is represented as independent commerce/payment obligations becoming a controlled, resolved state. The marks avoid literal blockchain and payment imagery and are designed to be judged as silhouettes before styling.

## 2. Source brand decisions

The source is [`SETTLE_BRAND_IDENTITY_DISCOVERY.md`](../SETTLE_BRAND_IDENTITY_DISCOVERY.md). Its primary territory is Convergent Ledger; Deterministic Branch is retained only as a complementary product visual grammar. The source principles are precision, trust, visible state, controlled energy, finality, and mechanism over cliché. Prohibited motifs include generic S forms, arrows, coins, shields, checkmarks, chain links, network nodes, and copied Circle/Arc/USDC geometry.

## 3. Shared geometry system

All candidates use a 24×24 internal grid, flat geometric fills, mostly 3–4 unit structural weights, hard orthogonal edges, and a short right-side resolved boundary. The common device is a terminal register/alignment edge rather than an arrowhead. Geometry is limited to two or three conceptual elements; negative space is intentional and no candidate depends on a gradient, stroke hairline, filter, or animation.

## 4. Candidate A — Axial Convergence

Two stepped independent paths enter a shared vertical terminal axis. The nested offsets imply convergence through alignment rather than directional arrows. It is the most literal expression of the territory and has strong reveal potential, but its stepped silhouette needs blind comparison against logistics marks.

## 5. Candidate B — Terminal Register

Offset ledger-like planes are compressed into a final bar. Compared with A, B is more measured and financial: the register is the primary event and the inputs are shorter. Its risk is that repeated horizontal bands may feel like a compact menu or database glyph at small sizes.

## 6. Candidate C — Settlement Notch

Two opposing solid planes define a central white passage. The negative-space notch is the settlement point: it is a controlled opening, not a hidden S. This is the strongest compact silhouette and the clearest meaningful negative-space candidate, though the central void must be checked for accidental shield/lock associations.

## 7. Candidate D — Balanced Convergence

Asymmetric stepped inputs resolve into a heavier, slightly shifted terminal. It carries controlled movement and a stable end state without mechanical symmetry. The asymmetry improves personality but makes optical centering essential at 24px and below.

## 8. Candidate E — Resolution Frame

Open planes appear incomplete until the right edge closes their relationship. It feels contained and institutional without an enclosing outline. This is the most “final state” oriented construction, with a risk of resembling accounting or document software when separated from the wordmark.

## 9. Candidate F — Ledger Alignment

Several minimal ledger strokes snap into repeated aligned positions and terminate at one bar. It provides the strongest infrastructure/developer vocabulary and the richest modular diagram relationship, but the repeated bands create the highest menu/list/equal-sign risk.

## 10. Negative-space analysis

C makes negative space structurally primary: the four-unit central passage represents alignment and controlled settlement. E uses the open interior between incomplete planes as a resolution field. A, B, D, and F use gaps as secondary separation so the elements do not merge. None uses a hidden-letter trick; the white spaces are functional relationships between planes.

## 11. Symmetry/asymmetry analysis

C is the most stable and bilateral. A and B are axis-led but offset internally. D is intentionally controlled-asymmetric and should be optically balanced rather than mathematically mirrored. E is edge-resolved with a contained rhythm. F uses modular repeated alignment rather than symmetry. The full set therefore tests stable symmetry, offset inputs, and asymmetric inputs with a resolved output.

## 12. Small-size test

The source SVGs are mathematically scalable and were reviewed as flat constructions at 128px, 48px, 32px, 24px, and 16px. The board [`SETTLE_MARK_SMALL_SIZE_TEST.svg`](./SETTLE_MARK_SMALL_SIZE_TEST.svg) aligns 48/32/24/16 previews for direct visual inspection. At 128px all path relationships are legible. At 48px all terminal devices survive. At 32px A, C, and D retain the clearest convergence; B, E, and F become more pattern-like. At 24px all silhouettes survive, but internal registration in F becomes dense. At 16px C has the strongest black/white read; A and D remain recognizable; B, E, and F are marginal because their repeated internal cuts compress.

No optical small-size variants were created in this exploration. That is deliberate: the marginal candidates need designer-led simplification rather than automatic path editing. A future C or A small variant should be tested only after the preferred geometry is selected.

## 14. Monochrome analysis

All marks are flat black on transparent/currentColor and use no opacity, gradient, glow, shadow, or filter. C has an explicit white knockout for its meaningful notch; the comparison board inverts that knockout for the dark field. The board shows black-on-white and white-on-black presentations. The marks remain structurally dependent on silhouette and spacing, not color.

## 15. Wordmark pairing

The exact product name is `Settle`. The neutral board uses a repository-safe Arial/Helvetica system sans as a subordinate text reference; no font is installed and no wordmark is converted to paths. Horizontal mark-left lockups are the default recommendation because the marks carry directional tension while the wordmark stays calm. Mark-above and mark-only pairings should be tested in the next review round. Future restrained opportunities include `tt` spacing and the relationship between `t`/`l` terminals and the mark register; no novelty typeface or modified letterform is recommended now.

## 16. Product-motif relationship

The separate Deterministic Branch / settlement rail can share the 3–4 unit path weight, hard terminal thickness, orthogonal corner treatment, and a common alignment edge. A can become the compressed convergence cue; B and F can inform evidence/register rails; C can provide threshold/passage geometry; D can express controlled input imbalance; E can inform resolved state framing. The logo must remain abstract and must not become the payout split diagram itself.

## 17. Motion potential

Each future reveal should be a single 600–900ms **CONVERGE / RESOLVE** sequence: independent elements begin slightly offset, move minimally on the grid, register against the terminal edge, then stop cleanly. A: stepped paths approach the axis. B: planes slide to the bar. C: opposing planes close toward the notch while preserving the passage. D: asymmetric inputs settle into optical balance. E: open planes complete their edge relationship. F: ledger strokes snap into one register. No loop, bounce, spring, fake speed, or animation is implemented here.

## 21. Preferred candidate

**Preferred for the next review round: A — Axial Convergence.** It best compresses “independent obligations become one resolved state” without relying on a literal symbol. This is not a final logo selection.

## 22. Fallback candidate

**Fallback: C — Settlement Notch.** It is the strongest small-size and monochrome fallback if A reads too much like routing/logistics. D should remain in the top-three review set as the distinctiveness challenger.

## 23. Optical small-size variants

No separate optical variant was created. The 16px gate identifies where a future variant may be justified: simplify B, E, or F only after external review confirms that the full construction fails. Do not automatically create six small identities.

## 24. What requires human visual review

Review the board blind at native rendered sizes on light and dark displays. Ask senior design reviewers to judge silhouette, originality, accidental category resemblance, wordmark proportion, negative-space clarity, and 16px raster behavior. Include print/deck scale, accessibility contrast, and a formal originality/trademark search. No candidate is approved by this document alone.

## 25. Recommendation for D4D0.6B2

Run an external critique of A, D, and C first. Refine only the selected construction and one fallback, including optical 16px variants, clear-space/minimum-size rules, and horizontal/stacked `Settle` lockups. Then test the chosen mark alongside a static Deterministic Branch settlement rail. Keep the mark flat and monochrome until the silhouette is approved; defer final brandkit production, metadata wiring, UI integration, and motion implementation.
